CAMPUS360 OPERATIONAL KNOWLEDGE
DOMAIN: ENERGY AND HVAC

TOPIC: High Energy Consumption

When abnormal or unusually high energy consumption is detected in a
building, administrators should investigate major energy-consuming
systems before changing operational settings.

Potential causes include:
- HVAC operating outside expected schedules
- Excessive cooling or heating demand
- Poor HVAC efficiency
- Simultaneous heating and cooling
- Lighting operating outside occupied hours
- High-load electrical equipment
- Equipment left running during low-occupancy periods

Recommended investigation sequence:

1. Verify the sensor reading and timestamp.
2. Compare current consumption with recent baseline consumption.
3. Check building occupancy.
4. Check HVAC operating schedules.
5. Inspect HVAC equipment for abnormal operation.
6. Check lighting schedules.
7. Identify unusually high-load equipment.
8. Compare current consumption with historical patterns.

If high consumption continues after verification, the facility operations
team should inspect the affected equipment and operating schedules.

HVAC optimization can reduce energy consumption when inefficient
operation, excessive cooling demand, scheduling problems, or unnecessary
operation are identified.

Evidence should be combined with:
- current energy consumption
- historical energy consumption
- occupancy
- anomaly score
- forecast
- equipment status

A recommendation should not be based only on a single sensor reading.
